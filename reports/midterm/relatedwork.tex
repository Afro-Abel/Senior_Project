De Mesentier Silva et al. use four agents with different playing styles to analyze Ticket to Ride maps and rule configurations \cite{silva2017}. Their work motivates studying multiple strategic preferences rather than relying on one general agent. This project applies that perspective to Backgammon and will compare both relative performance and measured behavior.

Tomasev et al. use AlphaZero to investigate small changes to chess rules and analyze their effects on gameplay \cite{tomasev2020}. Their study supports using automated play to explore rule consequences. This project differs in its stochastic game, smaller computational scope, and planned comparison of deliberately biased styles.

Browne et al. survey Monte Carlo Tree Search and its variants \cite{browne2012}. Their work provides context for simulation-based decision methods and the distinction between sampling and tree search. The present baseline uses equal-budget flat Monte Carlo evaluation, without UCT, adaptive tree expansion, or learned value networks. These sources motivate the methodology; they do not validate this project's hand-selected heuristic weights.
